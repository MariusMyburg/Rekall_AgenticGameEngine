## Early Access questions

### Why Early Access?

Rekall AGE introduces an unusual authoring workflow: people describe intent while AI agents operate through inspectable game-engine contracts, and both can continue editing the same project. Early Access lets us test that workflow against real games, hardware, local and cloud models, and different levels of development experience. Feedback will directly shape Studio usability, agent effectiveness, engine primitives, diagnostics, performance, and documentation.

### Approximately how long will this software be in Early Access?

We currently expect Rekall AGE to remain in Early Access for approximately 18–24 months. This is an estimate rather than a fixed deadline: the supported core must be reliable across real authored games and a broader range of Windows and Vulkan hardware before version 1.0.

### How will the full version differ from the Early Access version?

We plan to deepen the everyday Studio workflow, asset import and reimport, modeling and animation tools, 2D and 3D runtime breadth, performance and device recovery, accessibility, documentation, clean-machine installation, and release signing. Experimental browser, multiplayer, VR, and advanced rendering systems will be promoted only after they meet their own playable acceptance standards. Priorities may change as real authored games expose the most important generic engine gaps.

### What is the current state of the Early Access version?

The current release is a usable Windows-first Developer Preview. It includes project and scene authoring, hierarchy and typed Inspector editing, a Vulkan World viewport and Player, C# gameplay modules, local and cloud LLM adapters, CLI/MCP tools, validation and deterministic runtime inspection, physics, audio, animation, UI, shaders, materials, modeling primitives, packaging and audit workflows, comprehensive offline documentation, and 18 editable examples and testbeds. Some advanced integrations remain experimental, and users should expect rough edges and breaking workflow improvements during Early Access.

### Will the software be priced differently during and after Early Access?

Rekall AGE will launch in Early Access at **ZAR 500 in South Africa**. We may increase the price when the product reaches version 1.0 to reflect its broader capability and maturity. Any change will be communicated in advance.

Steamworks directive: set the South African price to exactly **R500 / ZAR 500**. Worldwide package prices remain unresolved until the Steam pricing/conversion UI is reviewed; do not invent or manually publish unreviewed regional amounts.

### How are you planning on involving the community?

We will use Steam Discussions and structured bug reports to collect feedback on successful and failed authoring prompts, Studio usability, hardware compatibility, model behavior, performance, documentation, and missing generic engine capabilities. Reproducible projects, diagnostics, screenshots, and deterministic input cases will help us turn individual game failures into reusable engine improvements.

## System requirements

These are conservative launch candidates. Confirm them on clean machines before store review; update the store if acceptance establishes different limits.

### Minimum

- Requires a 64-bit processor and operating system
- OS: Windows 10 64-bit
- Processor: x64 four-core processor
- Memory: 16 GB RAM
- Graphics: Vulkan-capable graphics device with current drivers
- Storage: 10 GB available space
- Additional notes: The .NET 10 SDK is required to author and build C# gameplay modules. Ollama and a separately downloaded tool-capable model are required for local AI authoring. Model memory and storage requirements vary substantially.

### Recommended

- Requires a 64-bit processor and operating system
- OS: Windows 11 64-bit
- Processor: modern x64 eight-core processor
- Memory: 32 GB RAM
- Graphics: dedicated Vulkan-capable GPU with 8 GB VRAM and current drivers
- Storage: 25 GB available space
- Additional notes: More memory, VRAM, and storage may be needed for large local language models, complex projects, high rendering quality, or experimental OpenXR workflows.
